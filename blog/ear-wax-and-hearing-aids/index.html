Ear Wax and Hearing Aids: Why Regular Checks Matter

14 October 20253 min read

Photo: hearing aid resting on a clean cloth

If you wear hearing aids, ear wax matters more than you might think. A small build-up that would go unnoticed in someone else's ear can stop a hearing aid from working properly.

How wax affects hearing aids

Wax can block the receiver, the tiny speaker that sits in or near your ear canal. When that happens, sound becomes weak, distorted or cuts out. Wax can also cause feedback, the whistling you sometimes hear from a hearing aid, and over time it can damage the device.

Why hearing aid wearers build up more wax

The ear canal normally cleans itself, with wax slowly working its way out. Wearing something in the canal every day can interrupt that process and push wax deeper.

Signs to watch for

  • Your hearing aids sound quieter or muffled than usual
  • More whistling or feedback
  • A blocked or full feeling in the ear
  • Visible wax on the dome or receiver when you take your aids out

How often to get checked

Many hearing aid wearers benefit from an ear check every six to twelve months, or sooner if you notice any of the signs above. We check your ears at your regular hearing aid reviews.

Safe, professional removal

Cotton buds push wax further in and can damage the ear canal. We remove wax by microsuction, a dry, gentle method performed under a microscope.

Due for an ear check?

Ear microsuction takes about 30 minutes.
